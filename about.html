Marginal Note

Why write in the margins?

This is an anonymous journal of essays about artificial intelligence, ethics, technology, and the world as it becomes.

The machine age is not a separate world. It is this world, under modification. The decisions being made now — who designs the systems, who is allowed to question them, whose work gets automated, what gets optimized and at what cost — are not technical decisions. They are moral ones, wearing technical clothing.

Most commentary on this topic comes with baggage: a company to market, a book to sell, a reputation to protect, a career to build. This site has none of that. There is no byline because there is no brand to serve. The argument stands or falls on its own merits, which is exactly how arguments should be judged.

What to expect

Essays, not hot takes. Written slowly, revised often, published when they're ready. Occasionally a note will be wrong; that is the point of thinking in public rather than performing certainty for an audience.

Topics rotate through:

  • AI — what the machines can and can't do, and how to tell the difference
  • Ethics — who benefits, who bears the cost, who gets to decide
  • Technology & society — how tools change the shape of attention, work, and power
  • The world — the human things that no model can capture, no matter how many parameters it has

House rules

No hype. No jargon deployed to impress. Every claim that matters gets a reason attached. No personality cult — the ideas are the point, and so the author stays out of the frame.

— Marginal Note

New notes are published as they are finished. There is no newsletter, no analytics, no tracking. If you find something worth passing on, pass it on.
